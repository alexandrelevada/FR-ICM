## FR-ICM: Fisher-Rao Weighted Iterated Conditional Modes
Pointwise classifiers predict labels independently, leaving relationships among neighboring samples unused at inference time. Although contextual refinement can exploit these relationships, uniform smoothing may propagate errors across class boundaries and overwrite reliable predictions. We propose a post-hoc label refinement method that incorporates Fisher-Rao geometry into a graph-based Potts model. Starting from a classifier’s predictions, the method estimates local class distributions on a nearest-neighbor graph and weights edges according to their Fisher-Rao distances, reducing interactions between statistically dissimilar neighborhoods. A hard-label fidelity term balances contextual agreement against preservation of the initial predictions. Labels are refined through sequential iterated conditional modes updates with fixed weights, ensuring non-increasing energy while keeping training labels clamped. The method requires no classifier retraining or probability estimates, and its regularization strength and Fisher-Rao bandwidth are selected through internal validation. Experiments on 22 datasets with six classifiers and 30 stratified random splits evaluate the method in a transductive setting against pointwise classification, nearest-neighbor smoothing, and unweighted ICM. The proposed method achieves the highest average balanced accuracy and macro-F1 for every classifier. Average balanced-accuracy gains over pointwise predictions reach 14.47 percentage points for Naive Bayes and 10.01 for decision trees, while gains over unweighted ICM range from 0.05 to 1.02 points across classifiers. With a KNN base classifier, Fisher-Rao refinement improves mean balanced accuracy over ICM on 21 datasets and ties on the remaining one. These findings support information-geometric weighting as a useful addition to contextual refinement, with benefits that depend on the initial classifier and dataset.
